How to Choose the Right Chart Type

July 2025

Mismatched chart types are the most common visualization mistake. The chart should answer the question, not just display the data.

Chart Selection Decision Tree

For comparisons: Use bar charts (horizontal for long labels). For trends: Use line charts. For distributions: Use histograms. For relationships: Use scatter plots.

When NOT to Use Each Chart

Don't use pie charts: For more than 5 categories, or when values are similar. Don't use 3D charts: They distort perception. Don't use stacked bars: When categories have very different scales.

The Key Principle

Match the chart to the question you are answering, not the data you have. A line chart shows a trend. A bar chart shows a comparison. A scatter plot shows a relationship.

Free vs Paid Courses: What Actually Matters

March 2025

The primary driver of learning outcomes is completion rate, not price. For foundational skills, free resources match or exceed paid alternatives.

Where Free Is Sufficient

SQL: Mode Analytics SQL Tutorial, LeetCode SQL. Python: pandas docs, Kaggle courses. Statistics: StatQuest, Khan Academy. Visualization: Data to Viz, Tufte resources.

Where Paid Delivers Value

1. Structured curriculum design. 2. Domain-specific tooling with proprietary interfaces (Tableau, Power BI). 3. Bootcamps with placement support.

Research Protocol Before Paying

1. Search for free version first. 2. Check completion reviews. 3. Define what you'll do within 48 hours. 4. Time-box to 6 weeks.

The Data Analyst Interview Playbook

March 2025

The data analyst interview is three or four different assessments: SQL screens, take-home/Python rounds, case studies, and behavioural interviews.

Round 1: SQL Screen

Tests: Can you think in sets? Practice window functions, self-joins, funnel analysis, and deduplication. Aim for LeetCode SQL Medium difficulty.

Round 2: Take-Home/Python

Tests: Can you work with messy data? Communicate findings. Deliver a report with clear findings, not just code.

Round 3: Case Study

Tests structured reasoning under ambiguity. Clarify before you hypothesise. Generate hypotheses systematically. The case study is where offers are won or lost.

4-Week Preparation Timeline

Week 1: SQL fundamentals. Week 2: Window functions. Week 3: Python/pandas EDA. Week 4: Case studies daily.

Avoiding Chartjunk: Design Rules for Data Visuals

May 2025

Edward Tufte's principle of maximizing the data-ink ratio still holds. Every pixel should serve a purpose.

The Data-Ink Ratio

Data-ink is the ink used to convey the data. Non-data ink (decorative elements, 3D effects, heavy gridlines) should be minimised.

Practical Rules

1. Remove unnecessary gridlines. 2. Avoid 3D effects. 3. Use colour intentionally. 4. Label axes directly. 5. Use white space effectively.

Building a Portfolio That Gets Noticed

April 2025

Most data analyst portfolios look the same. The key differentiator is solving real problems, not following tutorials.

The Forgettable Portfolio

Has projects sourced entirely from Kaggle competitions with pre-cleaned datasets, analysis that ends at model accuracy, and READMEs that describe what was done, not what was found.

What Hiring Managers Look At

The README (clear problem statement), code structure (evidence of organization), and the output (chart, table, dashboard viewable without running code).

Four Project Criteria

1. Dataset required work to get usable. 2. Question came from real context. 3. Analysis went somewhere with quantified findings. 4. You can explain every line in a 30-minute interview.

How to Prepare for Product Sense Questions

April 2025

Product sense questions have become standard in data analyst interviews at mid-to-large tech companies. They test whether you can define an ambiguous problem before trying to solve it, whether your reasoning is structured, and whether you understand the relationship between user behaviour and business outcomes.

The MECE Framework for Metric Design

MECE (Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive) helps you cover the relevant measurement space without overlap. Decompose the product into its core user journey, identify the value at each stage, and assign metrics accordingly.

The Three Question Types

1. "How would you measure the success of [product/feature]?" Clarify the goal first, then apply MECE hierarchy: awareness/discovery, engagement, quality, business outcome. Define your North Star metric and guardrail metrics.

2. "A metric has dropped. Walk me through how you'd investigate." Confirm the metric is real, define scope, hypothesise by category, and prioritize by magnitude and diagnosability.

3. "How would you evaluate whether we should build [feature]?" Start with the user problem, define the hypothesis explicitly, identify what you'd measure, and propose how you'd test it.

Key Takeaway

Spend the first 20-30% of your time clarifying the goal. The framework — problem → hypothesis → measurement → test design — is what separates analysts who can contribute to product decisions from those who only report what happened.
